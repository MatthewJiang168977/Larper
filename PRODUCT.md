# Larper: product

## Who it is for

People who like finding the odd, specific place near them: the hojicha soft serve in a mall
basement, a photobooth bar, the dumpling counter with no sign. They want one good idea, not a
list to scroll.

## The core loop

1. **Offered.** Each day Larper offers one side quest: the most niche spot nearby that fits the
   person's taste, with a few reasons why it was chosen.
2. **Accept, or pass.** They accept it, or say "not today" and, if they like, why.
3. **Go.** They walk there, on a 3D map of their surroundings.
4. **Check in.** Being there (within a distance) counts. Too far away says so and does not count.
5. **Learn.** Whether it was open, and what they did and skipped, feed back into the next pick.

The **journal** keeps every quest offered and how it went. From it the person can download their
data, delete their history, or delete their account, and each step asks before it acts.

## The voice

Quiet, specific and a little dry. Lowercase labels, plain sentences, no hype. It sounds like a
friend pointing at a door: "one quest a day, picked for you." It names the thing, not the feeling.
Errors say what happened and what to try next. The rules are in `DESIGN.md` under Copy.

## What Larper never does

Each of these is something the product already does, in code or on its privacy page.

- **Never sells data or uses it for ads.** The privacy page says nothing here is sold or used for ads.
- **Never claims more than the data supports.** "Why this place" lists only reasons the data backs
  up, and a shorter list beats a weak one. Opening hours say when they are unverified.
- **Never counts a check-in from too far away,** and says so plainly.
- **Never traps people.** Data can be downloaded and deleted from inside the app, deleting an
  account removes the sign-in too, and the app says what can remain in backups.
- **Never passes the shared demo account off as a real one.** The demo has no data of its own to
  download or delete, and says so.
